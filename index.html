[ Case Study — Fintech ]

Flow

AI-assisted personal budgeting, for people the banking apps forgot.

Personas Objectives Research Scope Architecture User Flows UX Writing Reflection

TL;DR

5 wks

Solo, end-to-end

3

Personas researched

12

User interviews

≥45

Target NPS

Situation — Budgeting apps show numbers; they don't build trust or explain the "why" behind advice.

Task — Design an AI-assisted budgeting system for users with irregular or tight incomes, shippable by a small team in 5 weeks.

Action — Ran discovery (interviews, diary study, survey), mapped 3 personas, scoped an MVP, and delivered a tested design system with tokens and dev-ready specs.

Result — A validated IA, a mid-fi prototype tested with real users (n=6), and a handoff-ready visual system targeting 35%+ Day-1 activation and 25%+ Week-4 retention.

Overview

Goal

Help 20–40 y/o users automatically track spending, forecast cashflow, and make smarter money decisions with an AI assistant.

Primary Outcomes

Activation, 12-week retention, and savings rate.

Platforms

iOS / Android, with responsive web to follow.

Constraints

Read-only banking via aggregator (Plaid-like); CSV import at MVP.

Users & Needs

AN

Anna, 28

Marketing Specialist

Lives paycheck to paycheck.

Needs clarity on finances and simple savings goals.

MK

Marek, 34

Freelancer

Has irregular income streams.

Needs cashflow forecasting and a dedicated tax bucket.

SR

Sara, 22

Student

Manages micro-budgets.

Needs real-time spending alerts and a clear "safe-to-spend" indicator.

Business Objectives & Success Metrics

North Star

Weekly Active Planners — users who set or track at least one goal weekly.

KPIs (targets set during scoping)

Day-1 Activation ≥ 35% Week-4 Retention ≥ 25% Savings Rate +5% / 3mo NPS ≥ 45 AI Chat CSAT ≥ 4.5 / 5

Competitive & Discovery Research

User Interviews

12 participants

Mix of individuals with regular and irregular income streams.

Diary Study

2 weeks · 8 participants

Participants logged daily "money moments" as they happened.

Survey

150 respondents

Explored budgeting habits, pain points, and signals of trust.

Key Hypotheses Validated

  • —Users prefer automated categorization over manual entry.
  • —Users want short-term cash flow forecasting, not just history.
  • —Users find value in actionable micro-nudges instead of spreadsheets.

Scope

MVP Features

Secure Onboarding Account Connect / Import Automatic Categorization Budgets by Category Alerts "Safe-to-Spend" View 30-Day Forecast AI Chat ("What if…?") Goals Vault Export CSV

Version 2 (Stretch)

Bill Detection & Negotiation Shared Budgets Credit Score Coach

Information Architecture

Flow is mobile-first, so navigation is a persistent bottom tab bar rather than a sidebar — the structural breakdown of the Home dashboard below reflects that.

Global Navigation

Bottom tab bar — Home, Budgets, Goals, Insights, Chat. Persistent across the app; active tab indicated by icon + label fill.

Primary Content (Home)

Balance header, Safe-to-Spend card, Upcoming Bills, Latest Transactions, Progress Toward Goals — stacked, scrollable, priority-ordered.

Contextual Layer

No persistent sidebar on mobile — contextual actions (edit rules, filters) surface as sheets anchored to the element they act on.

Home

Balance · Safe-to-spend · Bills

Budgets

Categories · Limits · Progress

Goals

Emergency Fund · Travel · Payoff

Insights

Trends · Merchants · Anomalies

Chat

NL Q&A · Quick Prompts

Core User Flows

Onboarding & Connect

1. Email / Apple / Google→ 2. Consent→ 3. Connect Bank or CSV→ 4. First Categorization→ 5. Set Top 3 Goals

Create Budget

1. Pick Categories→ 2. Auto-Suggest Limits→ 3. Confirm→ 4. Enable Alerts

Ask the AI

1. Type / Voice Question→ 2. Model Returns Forecast + Explanation→ 3. 1-Tap Action (adjust budget, set goal, remind)

UX Writing (Sample Copy)

Value Prop

“See your money clearly. Spend with confidence.”

Empty State

“Once you connect an account, we'll show your safe-to-spend and upcoming bills here.”

Error

“We couldn't connect to your bank right now. Try again or import a CSV.”

Privacy Note

“You're in control. Delete your data anytime in Settings.”

What I Learned

Financial design is trust wearing an interface.

  • —Connecting bank data creates real anxiety; transparency and clear onboarding reduced friction.
  • —Automation had to stay explainable — people accepted AI advice only when they understood the "why."
  • —Small micro-nudges outperformed complex dashboards for daily engagement.

Transparency about data — not more features — was what got people to connect their first account.

[ Case Study — Health & Habits ]

HabitNest+

Fitness, nutrition, and sleep — with personalization, for people who don't need one more spreadsheet.

Personas Objectives Research Scope Architecture User Flows UX Writing Reflection

TL;DR

4 wks

Solo, end-to-end

3

Personas researched

10

User interviews

≥70%

Target onboarding completion

Situation — Habit apps fail from friction and shame, not lack of features — most churn happens the first time someone misses a day.

Task — Design a habit-coaching system spanning fitness, food, and sleep that stays motivating after a bad week, not just a good one.

Action — Interviewed 10 users on adherence barriers and logging fatigue, tested 1-tap logging for speed, and designed failure states with deliberately kind copy.

Result — A tested mid-fi prototype (n=6) and handoff-ready visual system targeting 70%+ onboarding completion and 30%+ 8-week retention.

Overview

Goal

Build sustainable health routines combining habit coaching, food logging, and wearable sync with social accountability.

Outcomes

Increased weekly adherence; improved energy and sleep self-ratings.

Platforms

iOS / Android, with Apple Health & Google Fit sync.

Users & Needs

LN

Lana, 40

Busy Parent

Wants a clear daily plan, simple meals.

Needs low-effort guidance — no decisions left to make.

OL

Olek, 29

Beginner

New to structured fitness.

Needs motivation and visible streaks to stay engaged.

AD

Ada, 33

Runner

Already trains regularly.

Seeks structured plans and recovery insights to improve performance.

Business Objectives & Success Metrics

North Star

Weekly Adherent Users — people who complete at least 4 of 7 planned days.

KPIs (targets set during scoping)

Onboarding Completion ≥ 70% 8-Week Retention ≥ 30% Avg. Daily Steps +20% Sleep Score +10% Streak Median ≥ 6 days

Competitive & Discovery Research

User Interviews

10 participants

Explored adherence barriers, logging fatigue, and the social impact of tracking.

Usability Tests

Task-based

Could participants log an entry in under 30 seconds and clearly understand their plan?

Survey

200 respondents

Measured meal-logging tolerance, notification frequency, and reward sensitivity.

Assumption Validated

1-tap logging and "good-enough" estimates sustain engagement better than precise gram counts for non-athletes — benchmarked against MyFitnessPal, Cronometer, Nike Training Club, Strong, and Fabulous.

Scope

MVP Features

Personal Goals & Auto Plan Daily Plan (Workout + Habit + Meal) Micro-Circle Social (3–5 friends) Streaks, Badges & Reminders 1-Tap / Barcode / Photo Logging Apple Health & Google Fit Sync

Stretch

Coach Chat Custom Training Programs Recipe Builder Garmin / Polar Integration

Information Architecture

Mobile-first with a persistent bottom tab bar — six tabs, each scoped to one job.

Today

Checklist · Streak · Reminders

Log

Food · Water · Weight · Mood

Train

Plans · Timers

Eat

Meal Ideas · Grocery List

Progress

Trends · Achievements

Social

Circle Feed · Challenges

Core User Flows

Onboarding

1. Goal→ 2. Level→ 3. Dietary Prefs→ 4. Time Windows→ 5. Enable Sync→ 6. Schedule Reminders

Daily Log

1. Open Today→ 2. Tap Workout→ 3. Follow Timer / Video→ 4. Mark Done→ 5. Log Meal + Water & Sleep

Create Circle

1. Invite Contacts→ 2. Accept→ 3. Share Weekly Challenge

UX Writing (Sample Copy)

Value Prop

“Small wins, every day.”

Today, Empty

“Your plan appears here at 6:00 each morning.”

Workout

“Short on time? Do the 7-minute reset.”

Streak Loss

“Streak paused, not lost. Start again today.”

What I Learned

Designing for human psychology matters as much as designing for functionality.

  • —Users valued quick logging over accuracy — the less effort, the higher the adherence.
  • —Motivation is social: even a small private circle of friends made accountability powerful.
  • —Designing "failure states" (streak loss) with kind, encouraging copy improved user morale.

The less a habit costs to log, the longer it survives contact with a bad week.

[ Case Study — Smart City & Travel ]

CitySense

A map, an AR layer, and a sense of place for travelers drowning in options.

Personas Objectives Research Scope Architecture User Flows UX Writing Reflection

TL;DR

4 wks

Solo, end-to-end

3

Personas researched

6

Field-test users

≥40%

Target place-save rate

Situation — Travel apps drown users in listings instead of narrowing to what actually fits the moment.

Task — Design a map-first, AR-assisted discovery tool that builds sharable itineraries without demanding a full itinerary upfront.

Action — Shadowed travelers planning real trips, ran contextual inquiry at hotels/transit, and prototyped a mood- and weather-aware discovery feed tested with 6 field users.

Result — A validated IA and a mid-fi prototype targeting 40%+ place-save rate and 30%+ AR usage per session.

Overview

Goal

Help travelers discover places that match interests, mood, and weather; build sharable mini-itineraries; light booking.

Outcomes

More saved places, itinerary completes, and merchant clicks.

Users & Needs

IR

Irina, 26

Weekend Traveler

Trip-hops on short breaks.

Seeks photogenic spots, coffee places, and thrift stores.

PT

Piotr, 37

Foodie on Business Trips

Books last-minute, works late.

Needs late-night dining options and reliable offline maps.

ND

Nadia, 31

Planner

Plans trips weeks in advance.

Wants structured daily itineraries and tools for time budgeting.

Business Objectives & Success Metrics

North Star

Itinerary Completion Rate — at least one saved plan with five or more points of interest.

KPIs (targets set during scoping)

Place Save Rate ≥ 40% Share Rate ≥ 15% AR Usage ≥ 30% of sessions

Competitive & Discovery Research

Shadowing

6 participants

Observed how people created and followed real city day plans.

Contextual Inquiry

Field study

Studied behaviors at hotel lobbies and key transit touchpoints.

Survey

200 respondents

Explored planning horizons and filters like budget, kid-friendly, and accessibility.

Key Hypothesis

Mood- and weather-aware suggestions reduce planning time; a map-first UI increases engagement — benchmarked against Google Maps, TripAdvisor, Culture Trip, and GetYourGuide.

Scope

MVP Features

Map-First Discovery (Mood Chips) AR Peek Light Booking (Merchant Deep-Links) Offline Pack Itinerary Builder (Drag & Drop)

Stretch

Collaborative Itineraries AI Chat ("Plan My Afternoon") Transit Tickets

Information Architecture

Mobile-first with a persistent bottom tab bar — five tabs built around discovery, not a sidebar full of settings.

Discover

Personalized feed

Map

Filters · Weather

AR

Camera overlay of POIs

Plan

Timeline · Walking times

Saved

Lists · Share/Export

Core User Flows

Onboarding Interests

1. Pick Interests + Budget + Walking Tolerance→ 2. Allow Location

Build Plan

1. Discover→ 2. Save→ 3. Plan Timeline Auto-Fills→ 4. Adjust Order→ 5. Share

AR Quick Look

1. Open AR→ 2. See Closest 5 Spots→ 3. Tap to Detail→ 4. Add to Plan

UX Writing (Sample Copy)

Value Prop

“Find places that fit your vibe.”

POI Detail

“Open now • 5-min walk • Quiet in afternoons.”

AR Hint

“Point toward a street — CitySense labels what's ahead.”

Share Text

“I planned a day in Kraków — follow my CitySense map.”

What I Learned

Context is everything in travel UX.

  • —Weather- and mood-based suggestions built more trust than static lists.
  • —AR was exciting but only valuable kept simple — a quick peek, not a long session.
  • —Offline access was critical; travelers needed confidence the app worked without signal.

Travel products succeed when they adapt to the moment and reduce uncertainty for the user.
